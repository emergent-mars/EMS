**National Registry Drugs**

`Aspirin` (ASA)
> Bayer, Ecotrin
> Dose: **324mg** chewed and swallowed. (PO)
Inhibits platelet aggregation (anti clotting)
* Indications: Suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS) or myocardial infarction (MI), *cardiac related* chest pain/discomfort
* Contraindications: GI bleed, allergy to ASA, unable to swallow, <16yo
* Side effects: heartburn

`Albuterol`
> Proventil, Ventolin
> Dose: SVN (small volume nebulizer) 6-8LPM with O2/**2.5mg** in 3ml saline;  MDI/**Two puffs**
Selective beta-2 adrenergic receptor agonist, smooth muscle relaxant, bronchodilation.
* Indications: Respiratory distress due to bronchial asthma or COPD
* Contraindications: Severe tachycardia
* Side effects: Increased HR, tremors, palpitations
g++ 
`Epinephrine`
> Epi-pen, Epi-pen jr, Twinject
> Dose: Adults (>66lbs) **0.3mg** IM, peds (<66lbs) **0.15mg** IM. In the middle of the vastus lateralis. Can repeat dose after 15min if it is not working or symptoms return, but call doc first.
Bronchodilation, vasoconstriction
* Indications: Anaphylactic w respiratory distress/airway comp and/or hypoperfusion.
* Contraindications: *None* in a life-threatening event
* Side effects: Increased HR and breathing, hypertension, anxiety, increased MVO2/CP

`Nalaxone`
> Narcan
> Dose: **0.5mg** IN/IV and titrate to effect, up to **2mg total**
Competitive opioid receptor antagonist.
* Indications: opioid-induced CNS and respiratory depression, coma of unknown origin with impaired airway or respiratory depression. *Suspected* OD.
* Contraindications: None
* Side effects: Increased HR, nausea/vomiting, pulmonary edema, agitation/withdrawal symptoms
>> Signs of OD: Altered mental status/unconscious, pinpoint pupils (bilateral), respiratory depression. Trying only to get them breathing on their own, don't need to wake them up.

`Nitroglycerin`
> Nitrostat, Nitro-bid
> Dose: One **0.4mg** tablet or spray sublingually every 5min, PRN if chest pain/discomfort persists to a max of 3 doses (**max total 1.2mg**)
Dilates blood vessels, decreases workload of the heart, decreases cardiac oxygen demand (MVO2). a "crisp" light-sensitive tablet, if not crisp check expiry date.
* Indications: Suspected ACS/MI, *cardiac related* chest pain/discomfort
* Contraindications: Systolic BP below 100 mmHg, max dose taken PTA, taken ED meds in previous 24 hours (viagra, cialis, levitra), BP drop of 30 mmHg from initial
* Side effects: Hypotension, headache, syncope
>> When you call physician to give dose, ask ahead to give up to 3x, otherwise you have to call per dose.

`Oral Glucose`
> Glucose. insta-glucose
> Dose: 15g buccally (between cheek and gums, but tbh just have them suck it down)
Increase blood glucose levels.
* Indications: Known or suspected hypoglycemia. It takes longer for hyperglycemia to hurt you than hypoglycemia so if unsure, give it.
* Contraindications: Unable to swallow, unable to take PO meds for another reason
* Side effects: None if given properly

**Denver Metro Meds**

`Acetaminophen`
> Tylenol
> Dose: Adults 1000mg PO, Peds 15mg/kg PO
Elevates pain threshold and readjusts hypothalamic temperature-regulatory center.
* Indications: Mild pain, moderate, or severe pain; fever (>38.3 C / 101 F)
* Contraindications: Allergy to acetaminophen, chronic liver disease, theraputic dose of acetaminophen within the past 6 hours, greater than 3 G in the last 24 hours
* Side effects: Nausea and vomiting, consider hepatotoxicity in OD or chronic alcohol use

`Ibuprofen`
> Motrin, advil
> Dose: Adults 600 mg PO, Peds 10 mg/kg PO
Decrease pain and inflammation by several mechanisms. NSAID.
* Indications: Acute treatment of mild, moderate, or severe pain; pain due to suspected kidney stones, acute exacerbations of chronic pain, musculoskeletal pain, fever
* Contraindications: Allergy to NSAIDs including aspirin and naprosyn, aleve; pregnancy or breastfeeding; history of GI bleeding or active stomach ulcer; history of chronic kidney disease or kidney transplant; anti-coagulation/anti-platelet (bloodthinners) or history of blood clotting disorder
* Side effects: Allergic reactions (anaphylaxis, urticaria, angioedema, bronchospasm, rash, hypotension), nausea and vomiting, GI Bleeding with chronic use, acute kidney injury

`Ipratropium Bromide`
> Atrovent
> Dose: 2y-adult 0.5mg with albuterol in a nebulizer, *not indicated for repetitive dose or continuous neb use*
Anticholinergic bronchodilator chemically related to atropine.
* Indications: Bronchospasm
* Contraindications: Soy or peanut allergy or the metered dose inhaler not nebulized solution because its in the propellant.
* Side effects: Palpitations, tremors, dry mouth

`Ondansetron`
> Zofran
> Dose: Adult 4mg ODT, may repeat x1; Peds 6mo-4yo 2mg ODT
Anti-emetic, decreases nausea/vomiting.
* Indications: Nausea/vomiting
* Contraindications: Use with caution with first trimester of pregnancy.
* Side effects: None if administered properly.

`Phenylephrine`
> Neo-synephrine
> Dose: First, have patient blow their nose to expel clots, then, administer 2 sprays into affected nostrils, pinch the fleshy parts of the nose.
Vasoconstriction of the nasal mucosa, decrease nasal bleeding.
* Indications: Epitaxis (nosebleed)
* Contraindications: None
* Side effects: Nasal dryness, burning or stinging (avoid contact with the eyes)

# Respiratory Emergencies
**Normal breathing**
* Stimulation to breathe is CO2
* Diaphragm active contraction on inspiration, relaxation on expiration
* 1-2 in visible rise and fall is normal

Respiratory...
> Distress -- difficulty breathing, can resolve on its own
> Failure -- not breathing effectively, on its way to arrest, seeing changes in physiology
> Arrest -- not breathing at all

`Dyspnea`: difficulty breathing
> Mechanical disruption to airway, lungs, or chest wall. Help ventilate
> Inadequate gas exchange via perfusion disturbance (CO, cyanide)
`Agonal`: decreased rate, gasping breathing
`Apnea`: not breathing
`Bradypnea`: slow breathing
`Tachypnea`: fast breathing
`Hypoxia`: lack of O2
`Hypoxemia`: lack of O2 in the blood
`Hypercapnia`: Elevated CO2
`Bronchoconstriction`: narrowing of bronchus
`Bronchodilation`: widening of bronchus
`Tripod position`: leaning over with hands braced on knees, sign of respiratory distress

`Pulse Ox`
Too much O2 can be harmful.
In cases of cardiac chest pain, stroke, and ROSC -- need to use the 94% SpO2% measurement to guide if you give O2-- >94% don't give O2.
ROSC = heart starts beating on its own after arrest
Class 1 - conditions for which there is evidence, general agreement, or both that a given procedure or treatment is useful and effective.

*O2 should be administered to the patient suspected of ACS if they are dyspneic, hypoxemic, has obvious signs of heart failure, or has a spO2 <90%*

`Airway`
> Visualize for any type of foreign body in the airway, when/how to remove. Finger sweep, suction, get ALS.
If they are awake and talking, they have an open airway.

PPV == ventilating for someone

iGel (supraglottic) invasive airway tube, 3 lengths.
4 = 4.5-5'
5 = 5-6'
6 = 6'+

CPAP, continuous positive airway pressure, "air splint" keeps some of the air in a patient's lungs when they exhale so it doesn't collapse. starting pressure is 0.5 mmH2O.

Rate and Tidal vol combo
> If adequate, provide appropriate O2
> In inadequate, provide PPV w O2 immediately
> Cyanosis is usually inadequate--AMS can be deciding factory
Subjective decision from objective info, use assessment techniques to determine a course of action.

Establish airway, PPV, emergent transfer.
If patient is found to be a priority, consider ALS backup and expedite transport.

If adequate, monitor, O2 if appropriate, MDI/nebulizer/humidified O2, complete history + physical exam, place patient in a position of comfort--typically a fowler's or semi-fowler's, then begin transport.

**Capnography + ETCO2**
ETCO2 = end tidal CO2, measures the amount of CO2 that is expired. measured with a capnograph
Normal range is 35-45mmHg, in Colorado 30-45mmHg
High ETCO2 can occur when air is held in the lungs for longer than usual (respiratory depression, slow RR), and low can occur when hyperventilating.

Looking at both ventilation rate and exchange capability
Rate: hypoventilation raises ETCO2, hyperventilation lowers
Exchange: damage to alveoli that limits gas exchange, lowers ETCO2

> Signs: fever, cough, dyspnea, tachypnea, tachycardia, abnormal breath sounds, diaphoresis, cyanosis and low pulse ox in some cases.
> Symptoms: Malaise, decreased appetite, sharp localized chest pain.
> Treatment: humidified O2, to the hospital, or they may refuse and go to PCP

`Pulmonary Embolism` **PE**
An obstruction of blood flow in the pulmonary arteries that prevents blood from reaching the lungs for oxygenation. Risks for developing PE include sedentary position, long bone fractures, smoking, and birth control. Things that increase the likelihood of a clot (ie DVT) forming and traveling to the lungs.
> Signs: cyanosis, hypoxia, tachypnea, tachycardia, falling BP, ETCO2 decrease, cool clammy skin, distended neck vein, cough (50% cases). Still would have clear lung sounds.
> Symptoms: restlessness/anxiety, sharp localized pain.
> Treatment: Get to the hospital ASAP, O2 for hypoxia but you can't save them.

`Pulmonary Edema`
Acute pulmonary edema as part of congestive heart failure (CHF). Buildup of fluid.
> Signs: Dyspnea, orthopnea (difficulty breathing dependent on position), frothy sputum, tachycardia, tachypnea, tripod position, crackles and possible wheezing, distended neck veins, possible peripheral edema.
> Symptoms: fatigue, anxiety or confusion.
> Treatment:

CHF you can tell which side of the heart failure is on, left heart causes pulmonary edema; right heart causes peripheral edema.

`Pneumothorax`
Spontaneous (bleb ruptures), traumatic, hemothorax, tension (putting pressure on the heart and other lung).
Air, blood, or other fluids leaking into pleural space and compressing the lungs.
> Signs: decreased breath sounds to one side, possible subcutaneous emphysema, tachypnea, tachycardia, pallor or cyanosis, deteriorating pulse ox.
> Symptoms: sudden onset shortness of breath, sudden onset of *sharp* chest or shoulder pain
> Treatment: Use a needle to drain if BP is hypotensive or systolic < 100, occlusive bandage for open traumatic pneumothorax, if closed pneumothorax give high flow O2 and call ALS.

`Hyperventilation`
Caused by nervousness and anxiety, emotionally charged condition, patient caught in a hyperventilation-hypoxia loop.
> Signs: dyspnea, tachypnea, tachycardia, hand and feet spasm
> Symptoms: dizziness, short of breath
Treatment: Need to relax patient with coaching, maybe explain what is happening physiologically to show you know what you're doing. They don't really need actual meds or treatment but O2 can be sort of a placebo. Do not tell them to calm down.

> Conductivity - transmit stimulation to other cells (dromotropy)

# Shock

**Recognition**
- Hypoperfusion;  State of inadequate tissue perfusion.
Cells need O2 and nutrients, and need to get rid of waste products.
^ Anytime there is a breakdown in *either* of these, that is shock.

**Causes of Shock**
Mnemonic: SHRIMP CAN
`S` eptic
`H` ypovolemic
`R` espiratory
`I` nsulin ::  a problem getting glucose into cells, a type of metabolic
`M` etabolic :: cells don't have the ability to produce energy because lacking nutrients
`P` sychogenic

`C` ardiogenic
`A` naphylactic
`N` eurogenic

> Physiology of perfusion
Normal perfusion includes a pump piping fluid through pipes into tissue.

` Pump Problems `
CO decreases due to affected stroke volume or HR
- Cardiac arrest, arrhythmias

` Pipe Problem `
Container problem, requires more fluid to deliver same amount of nutrients.

` Fluid Problem `
Container breaks, losing blood, lack of fluid.
Buildup of toxins or lack of O2 carrying capacity.

**Phases of Shock**
1. Initial stage
- Hypoxia

2. Compensated
- Acidosis -> hyperventilation
- epinephrine and norepinephrine increase HR and BP a small amount
- blood diverted to vital organs
- low urine output

3. Decompensated/progressive (ALOC - altered level of consciousness)
- compensatory mechanisms fail
- pre-capillary sphincters relax and blood starts to pool in capillaries.\
 - mottled skin

4. Irreversible/Refractory
- Vital organ failure and death
 - They will die without constant treatment
- ATP breaks down into adenosine and will not be produced fast enough to restore ATP

` Timeline of ALOC `
Goes backwards from AAOx4

` MAP = (2D + S) / 3 `
ie for a HR @ 80 BPM :: 120/80 = (2*80+120)/3 = (160 + 120)/3 = 280/3 = 93.3

Need MAP > 60 to perfuse brain and kidneys
- Acute kidney injury starts in 20min w MAP<60. 'normal' 70-110.

Can include this in a radio or hospital report.

**Shock Treatment Plan**
1. Administer O2
2. Avoid excess heat loss (clotting cascade)
3. Position of comfort
- Elevate legs 10-12in 
(a lil useful in decompensated progressing into refractory shock, limited benefit)
4. Monitor vital signs
5. Transport rapidly
6. ALS for fluid bolus
- permissive hypotension.

**Allergic Reactions**
- Antigen: any foreign substance that gets into the body
- Antibody: produced by the body in response to the antigen for the body to fight it
- Allergen: antigen this capable of produces an allergic reaction
- Allergic reaction: localized reaction to an allergen
- Anaphylaxis: systemic reaction to an allergen
    - anaphylactic shock is when it affects lungs and heart

Antigens enter the body through injection, inhalation, ingestion, and absorption.
Injection -- needle, stinger, fangs
Inhalation -- through absorption in respiratory system
Ingestion -- through absorption in the GI system
Absorption -- through absorption in the skin

Process
Body is exposed to antigen, WBCs signal production of an antibody that is specific to an antigen called sensitization. Mast cells with IgE attach to allergen and trigger the release of histamine.

Histamine dilates blood vessels and makes them abnormally permeable, which also causes vessels to leak fluid. Leaking fluid results in inflammation, warming and irritation of the skin, itching, watery eyes.

Urticaria (hives) -- dark red, raised, itchy bumps.
Edema (angioedema), which can cause airway swelling.

Anaphylactic Shock:
- ALOC
- BP<90

Treatment:
- For allergic reactions (EMT-B): Transport, oxygen, airway management.
    - At EMT-I/P level, can give antihistamines.
- For anaphylaxis (EMT-B): Transport, epi, o2 + airway management. with iv cert, draw it up yourself
